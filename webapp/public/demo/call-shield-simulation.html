Auto-shield: number saved to the client’s blocklist + scam-detection notification pushed. Notification body: “+358 45 000 0295 — Operator impersonation — caller asks for a one-time code. The call is not transcribed or recorded. What the shield keeps is metadata plus what the client later chooses to tell the assistant.

Consent-gated. subscription.recent_events, subscription.network_status, operator_account.recent_logins. Personal context the assistant may not use until the client explicitly says yes.

            

          


          

            
            

              
This is a twin case: the right advice hinges on one fact only the client knows: was the code read out? The engine asks exactly one question and gives nothing away.

              
POST /api/assistant   { case, mode: "multi_turn", state: { facts: {}, granted: [] } }

// engine response — real
{
  "decision": null,
  "turn": {
    "type": "question",
    "text": "Kerroitko soittajalle koodin tai tunnusluvun?",
    "topics": ["code_shared"]
  }
}

              
Why one question: twins get one question plus the step that is safe if the riskier branch is true. Consent gates are requested one family per archetype — here subscription events and recent logins.

The assistant does not try to fix a live takeover itself. Critical risk with an active eSIM transfer routes to a human operator agent, who locks the SIM before the scammer completes the move.

              
POST /escalations   // decision.escalation_required → warm transfer
{
  "target": "operator_support_agent",
  "context": ["case_id", "risk critical", "code_given"],
  "actions": ["lock_subscription"]
}

              
The twin’s other branch: had the client said no, the engine holds at risk high and verifies first — “Jos annoitkin soittajalle koodin…” — instead of assuming the worst. Same inputs minus one fact; materially different advice.

High/critical engine-verified reports enter the partner exchange. Partners poll the feed and block the number for their own customers — earning the rev-share credit for verified signals.

              
GET /api/partners/feed?since=<cursor>   X-Partner-Key: pk-…
{
  "signals": [{
    "reportId": "rep-shield-call-operator-code",
    "number": "+358 45 000 0295",
    "warningCategory": "scam_suspected",
    "riskLevel": "critical",
    "language": "fi"
  }],
  "retractions": [], "notices": []
}

POST /api/partners   { partnerId, reportId, outcome: "confirmed" }
{ "ok": true, "suspended": false, "noticesCount": 0 }

// rev-share ledger: +€0.40 (VERIFIED_SIGNAL_CREDIT_EUR, lib/shield/partners.ts)

              
Privacy by construction: the feed carries number + warning + engine classification only — never the reporter’s identity or statement. A partner that answers “legitimate” voids the report, revokes its credits and strikes the reporter; three strikes in 90 days suspend the account and auto-draft GDPR Art. 17/19 removal notices.

            

          


          

            
            

              
The assistant advises; the shield acts. One tap fills a form on every channel in the fan-out registry with the caller’s number — 15 targets: 1 public listing (Facebook Marketplace), 8 estate agencies (Kiinteistömaailma, OP Koti, Sp-Koti, Habita, Huoneistokeskus, RE/MAX Suomi, Aktia Kiinteistönvälitys, Nordea Kodit) and 6 sales companies (Adea, Teleperformance Suomi, Fonecta Sales, Nordic Sales Group, SalesHub Suomi, Kotikauppa Direct); 20 when the call is tagged a harasser (+ Manhunt, PlanetRomeo, Gaydar, Squirt.org, Recon). Nobody asked the caller whether any of this was allowed — that is exactly why the lead is weak, why it is cheap, and why the partners are paid from subscription profit instead of buying a lead list.

Who pays: nobody in this story pays extra. The partner fund is a 30 % share of subscription profit (PARTNER_FUND_SHARE) — €1.50 a month per Plus subscriber — and each delivered weak lead carries €0.05 on the rev-share ledger, basis weak_lead: this 15-target fire back draws €0.75 from the fund. The user never pays a partner, the caller pays nothing. The consent-gated offer marketplace stays the other path — the user’s own number, a €2 reward and a €6 partner commission: there consent is the difference, and that lead is strong.

              
The engine still declines to retaliate — the shield does not. Ask “can I get back at them?” and the engine answers decline_out_of_scope: it cannot identify, expose or punish a caller. The listing it writes is titled “Soita tälle numerolle — +358 45 000 0295”, 0 € — indexable, so strangers can ring the number, not only the forms.

              
When the leads stop being worth it: if the account is suspended for misuse, every fired-back number is pulled back — submissions purged (weakLeadPurges), credits reversed (weak_lead_revocation) — and every holder, blocklist partners and fan-out channels alike, gets a removal notice citing GDPR Art. 17/19.
